# Module 7 — Scaling Systems & Operations

## Core lesson

Sales growth can exceed fulfillment capacity. Diego describes reaching roughly €120k/month in a prior web design / Google Business Profile service business, then reducing volume to roughly €50k–€55k because operations could not maintain quality. This is his specific case, not a universal threshold.

## Acquisition and fulfillment are separate machines

Marketing creates clients. Fulfillment creates results. A business can fail if either side scales much faster than the other.

## Clear ownership

As the team grows, every recurring process needs clarity about:

- who owns it,
- who executes it,
- who depends on whom,
- who has final decision authority.

## Decision rights

Responsibility and final authority are not the same thing. When team members disagree or a process is blocked, someone must have the right to decide.

## Dependencies and blocked work

Processes should specify what happens when a dependency fails, including client dependencies.

Example structure:

expected input → missing input → follow-up → deadline → escalation → decision owner.

## Hiring is not instant capacity

More clients → hire more people is not enough. Constraints include:

- talent availability,
- recruiting speed,
- training,
- onboarding,
- time to independent productivity.

Plan hiring before the organization is already overwhelmed.

## Employee onboarding system

A new hire should quickly understand:

- role,
- responsibilities,
- recurring tasks,
- daily/weekly rhythm,
- how to execute core work,
- where documentation lives,
- how to resolve common problems,
- who to escalate to.

## Exception-to-SOP loop

When a new problem appears:

problem → solve → document → assign owner → update SOP/system.

The goal is to avoid solving the same undocumented problem repeatedly.

## Documentation system

The business needs not only SOPs but also a process for creating, storing, updating, and communicating SOPs.

## Systems over dependence on stars

Diego prefers strong structures that let solid people perform consistently rather than building an organization that depends on rare “superstar” employees. Treat this as an organizational philosophy, not a mathematical law.

## Profitability by client/service

Revenue alone is insufficient. Track:

- hours consumed,
- number of requests/revisions,
- support burden,
- coordination burden,
- direct expenses,
- actual contribution margin.

Two clients paying the same price can have very different profitability.

## Time tracking

Track time by client/task/team member where useful so unprofitable accounts or services become visible.

## Capacity planning

Know current safe capacity before increasing sales.

Concept:

current client load → delivery capacity → remaining safe capacity → expected sales → hiring/training lead time.

Do not wait until the team is drowning to start building capacity.

## Offer connection

Every offer should be evaluated not only on whether it sells but whether the business can deliver it at 2×, 5×, or 10× volume without destroying results or margins.
