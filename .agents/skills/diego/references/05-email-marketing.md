# Module 5 — Email Marketing

## Main principle

Diego treats a worked email list as a central owned asset. “Worked” means the list has spent time/interacted with the business rather than merely existing as a database.

## Lead scoring

Example scoring used by Diego:

- open = +2,
- click = +6,
- reply = +8.

These weights are his implementation example, not a universal standard.

Purpose: identify contacts with stronger engagement/familiarity for prioritization and reactivation.

## List-building channels discussed

- content,
- ads,
- SEO,
- affiliates,
- influencer marketing,
- third-party communities,
- podcasts,
- masterclasses/events,
- other partnerships.

Diego frames content leads as often higher quality and paid acquisition as faster to scale.

## Capture email from audience activity

Examples in his ecosystem:

- Instagram → automation/ManyChat → opt-in,
- YouTube → description/CTA → opt-in,
- live events → keyword/automation → opt-in.

## Lead magnet determines list quality

A bad lead magnet can create the wrong email list. Email copy cannot fully compensate for a mismatch between what attracted the lead and what the business sells.

## Basic opt-in landing structure

1. Strong promise.
2. Subtitle reinforcing promise / reducing pain.
3. Clear benefits.
4. CTA.
5. Visual representation/tangibilization of the resource.
6. Thank-you / confirmation step.

Benchmarks mentioned by Diego:

- cold traffic: >30% opt-in,
- warm traffic: >40% opt-in.

Treat as his contextual benchmarks, not universal standards.

## Welcome flow decision

### High awareness / strong purchase intent / finite buying window
Use the first emails to address objections and move toward call/purchase.

### Lower awareness
“Buy time” by selling the value of spending more time with the business, especially through long-form content and useful emails.

## Objection-driven welcome emails

When pushing toward a call/purchase, Diego recommends addressing the main reasons the buyer would not purchase. If those objections are unknown, he treats that as a customer-understanding problem rather than an email problem.

## Nurturing flow (“flow of money”)

Architecture:

- General list = place to test new emails/angles.
- Nurturing flow = proven/validated emails only.

When a broadcast performs above his internal standard, it can be added to the evergreen nurturing flow so future leads receive increasingly proven messaging.

## Validation metrics mentioned

Market averages cited in the lesson:

- roughly 18–25% open,
- roughly 1% click.

His own validation thresholds mentioned:

- previously >40% open and >2% click,
- later raising toward ~45% open and ~2.5% click.

These should not be treated as universal industry standards.

## Mini-launch / suppressed demand

Diego uses periodic mini-launches to convert nurtured demand. He prefers:

- announcing a genuine upcoming price increase,
- adding temporary value/bonus,
- temporary payment options,

rather than simply discounting the core price.

## Subject-line / angle categories

He highlights:

- curiosity,
- controversy,
- morbid/attention-grabbing story,
- real case,
- counterintuitive angle,
- absurd/unexpected framing,
- executable/tactical result.

The body must still deliver on the promise.

## Problem × angle system

One customer problem can be written from many angles:

- client question,
- personal story,
- case study,
- industry mistake,
- curious fact/data,
- contrarian lesson,
- tactical how-to.

This creates many emails without inventing entirely new problems.

## Email as message-testing lab

- Winning subject → potential ad hook.
- Winning body → potential ad body.
- Winning ad → potential email angle.

## Email structure

Subject → first line → body → CTA.

Emails should be visually easy to read with short paragraphs and spacing.

Possible CTAs include:

- watch a video,
- book a call,
- click a resource,
- reply,
- send a keyword,
- follow.

## List hygiene

Diego describes suppressing contacts who have not opened for around 60 days and views unsubscribes as healthy when they remove uninterested contacts.

## Known contradiction

The transcript contains inconsistent frequency statements:

- one section says approximately 3–4 general-list emails per week,
- another sounds like 3–4 emails per day.

Do not silently reconcile this. Treat the durable principle as: send consistently at a frequency the audience demonstrates it values, and use engagement/quality as feedback.
