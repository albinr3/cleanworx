# Module 2 — Funnel Selection

## Funnel choice variables

Diego says funnel selection should account for:

- buyer type: B2C, lower B2B, higher B2B,
- buyer/company revenue or sophistication,
- awareness of the problem/solution,
- whether the offer has been validated,
- evidence from similar markets,
- available budget,
- delivery type: DFY, DWY, DIY.

## YouTube funnel as supporting layer

YouTube is described as a layer that can strengthen almost any funnel because prospects can research the provider before/after becoming a lead or booking a call.

Basic structure discussed:

YouTube → lead magnet or direct call CTA → form → VSL → call → confirmation page.

## VSL structure taught

Approximate structure:

1. Promise.
2. Unique mechanism.
3. Why previous attempts failed.
4. Why this mechanism is different/better.
5. Social proof.
6. Process.
7. Close / CTA.

## B2B content qualification

Diego emphasizes that B2B content should often be specific enough to imply the viewer is in the desired situation. Example logic: a topic about scaling a sales commission structure implies an existing sales team more strongly than a generic “build a sales team” topic.

## Dual CTA logic

Use both:

- lead magnet for people who need more nurturing,
- direct call CTA for people who already have enough conviction.

## Community / lead magnet funnel

Emphasized for B2C and lower B2B.

Typical structure:

Ad → lead magnet → email/phone → community → setter → call → confirmation → sales call.

A validated script/process should exist before delegating heavily to a setter.

## VSL without opt-in

Emphasized when:

- awareness is high,
- problem is specific,
- offer/market understands what is being sold,
- social proof / confidence is strong.

Lower friction increases the importance of call confirmation and pre-call nurturing because no-show risk can rise.

## VSL with opt-in

Used when more education/nurturing is needed. Diego makes a distinction between what the ad sells:

- With opt-in: often sell the mechanism/idea/curiosity that earns registration.
- Without opt-in: can sell the offer/problem more directly and let the VSL explain it.

## Phone number capture

Diego strongly prefers collecting the phone number when no other later mechanism will capture it, because direct access can be commercially valuable even if opt-in conversion is somewhat lower. Treat this as his preference, not a universal rule.

## Validation funnel / VSL ad

When offer or niche is not yet validated, Diego recommends testing with a simpler ad that functions as a mini-VSL instead of building multiple full funnels.

Concept:

2–2.5 minute offer ad → segment/niche variation → form or messages → call → attempt sale.

Important rule: choose winners primarily based on closed sales, not merely booked calls.

## Social / Instagram funnel

Ads / content → profile → stories/reels/DM automation → conversation → calendar → call.

Most strongly emphasized for B2C and lower B2B, especially when a strong Instagram audience already exists.

## Funnel selection shorthand

- Unvalidated offer/niche → validation funnel first.
- Lower B2B/B2C → community/lead magnet/social often emphasized.
- Higher awareness + specific B2B problem → VSL without opt-in can fit.
- Lower awareness → opt-in + nurturing.
- Add YouTube / long-form nurturing where useful.

These are source-framework tendencies, not universal deterministic rules.
