# Module 6 — High-Ticket Sales Calls

## Call stages

Diego describes a structured call with separate stages:

1. presentation/opening,
2. discovery/sondeo,
3. pitch,
4. close,
5. objection handling,
6. re-close.

Do not mix stages unnecessarily.

## High-attention environment

For higher-ticket sales, Diego prefers the prospect to take the call on a computer rather than casually on a phone because he wants attention and visual support. Treat the deeper principle as maximizing attention and seriousness; the “computer” rule is his implementation preference.

## Short pitch

Diego argues that excessive explanation increases cognitive load and delays decisions.

His approximate guidance:

- ~3–5 minutes is preferable,
- ~7 minutes as an upper bound in his system.

The pitch should explain only what the prospect needs to understand how the service solves the stated problem.

## Adapt pitch to discovery

Use the prospect's:

- stated problem,
- target outcome,
- current situation,
- relevant proof/comparables.

Framework stays consistent, content changes.

## Questions maintain direction

Diego's model treats the person asking questions as controlling the conversation. After answering a legitimate question, return to a relevant question/next step rather than letting the call become an unstructured interrogation.

## Vocal delivery

He emphasizes:

- projection,
- emphasis,
- pacing,
- variation,

as part of keeping attention and communicating certainty.

## Avoid generic flattery

Do not use vague praise simply to please the prospect. If recognition is appropriate, make it specific and earned.

## Do not automatically discount

Diego strongly discourages reducing price when pressured. He prefers questions that lead the prospect to articulate the difference between price and result/confidence.

## Do not assume objections

Do not assume a prospect cannot afford the main offer or wants a cheaper option without evidence.

## Root-objection framework

Diego reduces “real” objections to four categories:

1. I don't trust myself.
2. I don't trust you.
3. I don't trust the process/service.
4. I genuinely do not have the money.

Treat this as Diego's framework, not an objective universal law.

Common surface objections such as “I need to think,” “I need to talk to my partner,” or “I need to run the numbers” should be explored to find the specific decision barrier rather than automatically accepted or attacked.

## Objection isolation

Approximate structure:

1. Acknowledge the surface objection.
2. Confirm whether the prospect believes the solution can produce the desired result.
3. Ask why they believe that.
4. Ask what specifically prevents the decision.
5. Address the specific barrier.

## Rebut with questions

Diego generally prefers questions that make the prospect articulate their own reasoning instead of long argumentative rebuttals.

## Confirm before offering a concession

Before offering:

- payment plan,
- delayed start,
- special condition,
- bonus,

confirm that solving that specific issue would cause the prospect to move forward. If not, it was not the only objection.

## Sales-call audit checklist

- Is the environment focused?
- Is the pitch concise?
- Is it personalized to discovery?
- Is the seller maintaining structure?
- Is vocal delivery clear?
- Is praise genuine/specific?
- Is price being defended without panic-discounting?
- Are assumptions being avoided?
- Is the real objection isolated?
- Are questions used instead of speeches?
- Are concessions conditional on resolving the true barrier?
- Are stages kept separate?
